Why cheap land is cheap

Legal access, zoning, utilities, flood zone, wetlands. It is nearly always one of those five.

How we work

Check the five things

Vacant land is the easiest real estate to buy badly, because almost nothing about a parcel is visible from a listing or a map. A track running to the boundary is not legal access. A dry-looking lot can be mapped wetland. Zoning may permit nothing the buyer had in mind, and the nearest water main may be half a mile away.

Those five questions decide most of the value, and all five can be researched from public records before anyone spends money. Disclosing the answers — including the ones that reduce what a parcel is worth — is the difference between selling land and selling a problem.

What no seller should ever do is promise buildability. Whether a house can go up depends on the permitting authority, septic percolation and wetland delineation, none of which belong to the seller. A buyer who hears a guarantee should treat it as a reason to walk away and survey the parcel first.
